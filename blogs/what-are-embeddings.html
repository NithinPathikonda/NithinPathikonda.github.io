What Are Embeddings? The Fruit Map Guide to Vector Coordinates

By Nithin Pathikonda • November 2026 • 5 min read

The Core Idea: Computers cannot understand words, ideas, or feelings. They only understand numbers and mathematical formulas. An embedding is simply a GPS coordinate for meaning. It translates human concepts into numbers so that things with similar meanings end up close together.

🧒 Level 1: The 5-Year-Old Analogy (The Fruit Map)

Imagine a huge grid map laid out on the floor:

  • The horizontal axis (X) measures Sweetness (from 0 to 10).
  • The vertical axis (Y) measures Size (from 0 to 10).

Now, let's place objects on this map based on their properties:

  • Watermelon: High sweetness (8), Big size (9) → Coordinates: [8, 9]
  • Cantaloupe: High sweetness (7), Big size (8) → Coordinates: [7, 8]
  • Lemon: Low sweetness (1), Small size (2) → Coordinates: [1, 2]
  • Lime: Low sweetness (1), Small size (2) → Coordinates: [1, 2]
  • Sports Car: Zero sweetness (0), Huge size (10) → Coordinates: [0, 10]

Notice what naturally happened purely through geometry:

  1. Watermelon and Cantaloupe end up right next to each other on the map because they share similar fruit properties.
  2. Lemon and Lime are practically touching each other.
  3. The Sports Car is miles away from all the fruits.

Those coordinate numbers [8, 9] and [1, 2] are Embeddings!

📐 Level 2: Modern AI Embeddings (768 & 1,536 Dimensions)

In real-world Large Language Models (like OpenAI's text-embedding-3-small or Google's Gemini embeddings), instead of just 2 axes (Sweetness and Size), models use 768 or 1,536 dimensions!

Each dimension captures a subtle nuance of human meaning:

  • Is the statement formal or casual?
  • Is it related to software engineering, medicine, or cooking?
  • Is it expressing anger, joy, or curiosity?
  • Does the subject refer to an animal, a machine, or an abstract concept?

When you feed "The puppy chased the ball" into an embedding model, it outputs a list of 1,536 numbers:

[ 0.0142, -0.0431, 0.0892, 0.1201, -0.0053, ... 1,536 numbers ]

If you also embed "The dog ran after the toy", its 1,536 numbers will be almost identical, even though not a single word is the same!

🔍 Level 3: How Do Computers Compare Them? (Cosine Similarity)

How does a computer decide if two embeddings match? It calculates the angle between their coordinate arrows (Vectors). This is called Cosine Similarity.

  • Similarity = 1.0 (100% Match): Vectors point in the exact same direction.
  • Similarity = 0.0: Completely unrelated concepts (perpendicular).
  • Similarity = -1.0: Exact opposite meanings.

🏢 Level 4: Why Companies Use Embeddings in Production

Whenever you see embeddings used in enterprise applications, they almost always power one of three critical systems:

  1. Semantic Search & RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation):
    Traditional keyword search fails when a customer searches for "How do I fix my phone display?" and the help documentation says "Repairing smartphone screen". Embeddings allow search engines to match by meaning, finding the exact documentation paragraph to feed to an LLM.
  2. Vector Databases (ChromaDB, Pinecone, Qdrant):
    Storing millions of vectors and finding the nearest neighbors in sub-10 milliseconds.
  3. Clustering & Anomaly Detection:
    Automatically grouping thousands of customer support tickets or identifying weird, anomalous server logs.

💻 Level 5: Zero-Dependency Python Demo

Here is a 20-line pure Python demonstration of embeddings and cosine similarity with zero external libraries: 

import math

def cosine_similarity(v1, v2):
    dot_product = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(v1, v2))
    mag1 = math.sqrt(sum(a * a for a in v1))
    mag2 = math.sqrt(sum(b * b for b in v2))
    return dot_product / (mag1 * mag2)

# Simulated 3D coordinates: [Is_Pet, Is_Feline, Is_Vehicle]
cat   = [0.92, 0.98, 0.00]
tiger = [0.10, 0.99, 0.00]
car   = [0.00, 0.00, 0.95]

print("Cat vs Tiger (Both felines):", cosine_similarity(cat, tiger)) # ~0.72 (High)
print("Cat vs Sports Car (Unrelated):", cosine_similarity(cat, car))   # 0.00  (Zero)

💡 The Teacher's Takeaway

Next time someone mentions embeddings, don't get lost in mathematical intimidation. Remember: it's just a map. Words are pins on that map, and similar ideas sit on the same street.
